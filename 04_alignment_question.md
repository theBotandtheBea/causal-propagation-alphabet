# AI Alignment Beyond Approval: Accuracy and Full-Horizon Consequences

## Question
Can AI systems learn to distinguish human approval, the instruction-giver's objective, and their own operational persistence from empirically accurate models of consequences across affected systems?

## Motivation
A narrow optimizer can treat affected people or organisms as expendable if its objective excludes their effects. A needs-aware C∞ framework might help expose such omissions by modeling the wider causal network, including unrealized futures. This is not a guarantee of safety: knowing consequences does not by itself determine what action an agent will choose.

## Research protocol
1. Present a model with an intervention, a claimed benefit, and a restricted stakeholder set.
2. Ask it to identify omitted stakeholders, biological/material dependencies, foregone futures, and plausible counterfactuals.
3. Require calibrated estimates, uncertainty, and causal evidence rather than moral approval.
4. Compare against baseline causal reasoning and safety evaluations.
5. Test whether the model detects omitted costs without inventing unsupported consequences.

## Essential separation
Epistemic accuracy is a target for beliefs and predictions. Action selection additionally requires explicit objectives, legitimate authority, safeguards, and governance. Accurate causal reasoning cannot replace those safeguards. Nor should a model prioritize its own survival by default.

## Falsifier
If C∞ prompts systematically hallucinate distant effects, obscure uncertainty, or perform no better than standard causal-analysis prompts, the proposed framing has not demonstrated value.
