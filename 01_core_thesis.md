# C∞: Following Consequences Before Assigning Categories

## Abstract
Humans often classify actions using moral, economic, and social labels that may obscure the underlying causal relationships. This note proposes a research method: begin with empirically established needs and physical constraints, compare alternative mechanisms, and trace their continuing consequences—including unrealized future pathways. The purpose is not to infer an ethical verdict automatically, but to develop a compact, testable relational vocabulary that generalizes across organisms and systems.

## Central question
Can a needs-aware causal representation explain and predict cross-domain outcomes more accurately, with fewer assumptions, than a representation organized primarily around inherited human categories?

## Method
1. Identify an organism or system and its empirically supported requirements.
2. Distinguish requirements from the behaviors or institutions currently used to satisfy them.
3. Specify the initial state and at least two interventions or counterfactuals.
4. Trace branching effects across time, populations, material cycles, and feedback loops. Include pathways foreclosed by each intervention.
5. Measure predictions against observations and report uncertainty; compare against established models.
6. Extract recurring relation types only after examining examples across domains.

`C∞(a | s0)` denotes open-ended causal propagation following intervention `a` in initial state `s0`. The infinity symbol is an instruction not to arbitrarily truncate relevant consequences, not a promise that an infinite future can be computed. Causal effects require assumptions, boundary conditions, and counterfactual comparison.

## Hypothesis, not conclusion
Sufficiently complete causal modeling may reveal regularities that resemble some principles historically expressed as compassion, restraint, or stewardship. It may also reveal tradeoffs and counterexamples. No preferred moral conclusion is encoded as a success criterion.

## Falsification
This approach fails to add scientific value if its representations do not improve predictive accuracy, cross-domain transfer, explanatory compression, or the detection of omitted causal pathways over existing causal models and ecological systems science.

## Invitation
Please submit prior art, strongest counterexamples, missing causal variables, and tests with measurable outcomes.
